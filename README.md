# Parkinson-s-Disease-Longitudinal-Progression

Introduction
This assignment comes from a longitudinal cohort study investigating the progression of motor
symptoms in patients diagnosed with Parkinson's Disease (PD). The primary aim is to under
stand how motor symptom severity evolves over time and how this evolution is inuenced by
key patient characteristics such as sex, age at diagnosis, and speci c lifestyle factors. Motor
symptom severity is quantitatively assessed using the Uni ed Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale
(UPDRS) Part III: Motor Examination score. This score ranges from 0 to 108, where higher
values indicate more severe motor impairment.
The study enrolled 500 patients who were diagnosed with PD between 1995 and 2011. Mo
tor assessments were conducted at baseline and then annually for a total follow-up period of 7
years. For each participant, several variables were recorded, including the dates of each UPDRS
assessment, the corresponding UPDRS Part III score at each visit, and the patient's gender and
age at the time of diagnosis. Furthermore, their smoking history was thoroughly documented at
baseline. For the purpose of this assignment, we will focus on the inuence of smoking status on
the disease trajectory.
A common challenge in long-term observational studies like this is the presence of missing data,
often due to factors such as patient withdrawal, relocation, or inability to perform assessments
as the disease progresses.
